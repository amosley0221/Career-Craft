Expert Tips to Boost Your Career

Practical, actionable advice to help you navigate every stage of your professional journey.

Getting Started

How to Choose the Right Career Path

Choosing a career is one of life's biggest decisions, but it doesn't have to be overwhelming. Start with self-reflection.

Step 1: Know Yourself

Make a list of your strengths, interests, and values. What activities make you lose track of time? What problems do you love solving? What matters most to you in a work environment?

Step 2: Research Broadly

Don't limit yourself to careers you've heard of. Use our Career Explorer to discover paths you might not have considered. Talk to people in different fields — informational interviews are gold.

Step 3: Experiment

Before committing, try things out. Volunteer, freelance, take on side projects, or job-shadow. Real-world experience beats theoretical research every time.

Step 4: Start, Then Iterate

Your first career doesn't have to be your forever career. The average person changes careers 3-7 times in their lifetime. Pick a strong starting point and stay open to growth.

Networking

Networking Without Being Awkward

Networking doesn't have to mean forced small talk at stuffy events. Here's how to build genuine professional relationships.

Be Curious, Not Transactional

The best networkers aren't the ones who hand out the most business cards — they're the ones who ask the best questions. Show genuine interest in people's work and stories.

Start Online

If in-person events feel daunting, start by engaging on LinkedIn, Twitter/X, or industry forums. Comment thoughtfully on posts, share useful content, and join relevant groups.

The Coffee Chat Formula

Reach out to people you admire with a short, specific message: who you are, why you're reaching out, and a clear ask (usually a 15-20 minute chat). Keep it respectful of their time.

Follow Up and Give Back

After meeting someone, send a brief thank-you note. Look for ways to help them too — share an article they'd find interesting, make an introduction, or offer your skills. Networking is a two-way street.

Interviews

Ace Your Next Interview

Interviews can be nerve-wracking, but preparation turns anxiety into confidence. Here's your playbook.

Research Like a Detective

Go beyond the "About Us" page. Read recent news, understand their products, check Glassdoor reviews, and look up your interviewers on LinkedIn. The more context you have, the better your answers will be.

Master the STAR Method

Situation, Task, Action, Result. Structure your behavioral answers this way. Prepare 5-8 STAR stories that showcase different skills, and you'll be ready for almost any question.

Ask Great Questions

Your questions reveal your thinking. Ask about team culture, success metrics, challenges, and growth opportunities. Avoid asking things easily found on their website.

The Power of Practice

Record yourself answering common questions. It feels awkward, but it's the fastest way to improve. Do mock interviews with friends or use online platforms for practice rounds.

After the Interview

Send a thoughtful thank-you email within 24 hours. Reference something specific from your conversation. This small touch makes a big impression.

Salary

Negotiate Your Salary Like a Pro

Most people leave money on the table because they don't negotiate. Here's how to get what you're worth.

Know Your Market Value

Use sites like Glassdoor, Levels.fyi, Payscale, and LinkedIn Salary to research compensation for your role, location, and experience level. Data is your best ally.

Never Share Your Current Salary First

When asked about salary expectations, try: "I'd love to learn more about the role first. What's the budgeted range for this position?" Many locations have pay transparency laws on your side.

Negotiate the Total Package

Salary is just one piece. Consider signing bonuses, equity, PTO, remote work flexibility, professional development budgets, and title. Sometimes the best wins come from non-salary items.

Practice Your Script

Rehearse your negotiation conversation. A good template: "I'm really excited about this opportunity. Based on my research and experience, I was hoping for [range]. Is there flexibility there?"

Growth

Level Up: Continuous Career Growth

Landing the job is just the beginning. Here's how to keep growing and stay ahead in your field.

Invest in Skills, Not Just Titles

Focus on building transferable skills: communication, problem-solving, leadership, and technical proficiency. These compound over time and make you valuable in any role.

Find Mentors and Sponsors

A mentor gives advice. A sponsor advocates for you in rooms you're not in. Both are invaluable. Seek them out by doing great work and building genuine relationships.

Make Your Work Visible

Don't assume great work speaks for itself. Share wins in team meetings, write internal updates, and keep a "brag document" tracking your accomplishments. You'll need this for performance reviews and your next role.

Stay Adaptable

Industries evolve fast. Dedicate time to learning new tools, attending conferences, taking courses, and reading industry publications. The most successful professionals are perpetual learners.

Know When to Move On

Growth sometimes means changing teams, companies, or even careers. If you've stopped learning and there's no clear path forward, it might be time for a new chapter. That's not failure — it's strategy.

Getting Started

Making a Career Change at Any Age

It's never too late to reinvent yourself. Here's a practical guide to switching careers successfully.

Identify Transferable Skills

You have more relevant experience than you think. Project management, communication, analysis, leadership, and problem-solving transfer across every industry. Map your existing skills to your target field.

Bridge the Gap

Take online courses, earn certifications, or do freelance projects in your new field. You don't need a full degree — you need proof that you can do the work.

Build Your New Network

Join professional communities in your target industry. Attend meetups, join Slack groups, and connect with people who've made similar transitions. Their advice will be invaluable.

Reframe Your Story

Your career change is a strength, not a weakness. Craft a narrative that connects your past experience to your new direction. Diverse perspectives are incredibly valuable to employers.

Quick Wins

Bite-Sized Career Tips

01

Update your LinkedIn headline to say what you do, not just your job title. "I help companies scale through data-driven marketing" beats "Marketing Manager."

02

Use numbers in your resume. "Increased sales by 30%" is 10x more compelling than "Responsible for increasing sales."

03

Before any meeting, prepare one thoughtful question. It shows engagement and builds your reputation as a strategic thinker.

04

Keep a "wins journal." Write down accomplishments weekly. You'll be amazed how useful this is come review time or job search time.

05

The best time to negotiate salary is when you have leverage — a competing offer, a big win, or when they clearly want you. Don't negotiate out of desperation.

06

Learn to say no strategically. Taking on everything doesn't make you valuable — delivering excellent work on the right things does.
